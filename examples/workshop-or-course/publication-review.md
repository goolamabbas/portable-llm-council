## Recommendation

## What this run demonstrates

Five advisors, five fresh reviewers, and a separate chair returned analyses in this conversation. Advisors converged on paid validation before course production but differed on enrollment thresholds and how to approach buyers. Review added emphasis on repeat customer acquisition and interpreting weak sales. The chair incorporated a second-cohort condition and source tracking into its recommendation.

Reviewer preferences were E: 3 and A: 2; all five selected C as having the biggest blind spot. These counts describe reviewer preferences, not objective quality scores. The example shows limited strategic disagreement and meaningful disagreement over execution conditions.

## Qualifications to publish beside the example

1. **Funnel illustration.** The 32-click estimate assumes delivery to all 800 subscribers. Treating those clicks as 32 distinct sales-page visitors adds another assumption. Ten purchases divided by 32 is 31.25%, but neither that visitor count nor the required conversion rate was observed. Calling that conversion “unusually high” also introduces a benchmark the supplied scenario does not establish.
2. **Scheduling tension.** The workshop estimate is 22 hours against 24 hours over three weeks. An eight-hour first week spent on a seven-day presale leaves 16 hours. Feasibility depends on how much presale work is already included in the 18-hour preparation-and-administration estimate and how much preparation remains after the payment gate. The chair allows moving or cancelling the event, but does not provide a reconciled weekly schedule. It also carries the eight-hour limit into a six-month acquisition test even though the scenario fixes that availability only for the first six weeks.
3. **Refund terms.** The first step promises an automatic refund below ten buyers, while the change condition permits a second presale with funds remaining refundable. These can coexist if the first presale is refunded and buyers explicitly opt into a revised offer; that sequence is not stated. The earlier audit's unqualified consistency pass was too strong.
4. **Demand thresholds.** Ten payments, the six-to-nine revision band, and the fewer-than-six stopping rule are proposed judgments. The scenario does not validate those thresholds. Phrases such as “material refunds” and “manageable support” still need operational definitions.
5. **Execution evidence.** The conversation contains distinct dispatches and complete returns. The saved execution record preserves shared components and references rather than a standalone raw export of every dispatch. Model overrides were omitted; effective worker model identities were not confirmed. Fresh conversations were requested, but filesystem access was shared.
6. **Preservation fidelity.** The transcript reformats chair headings; the HTML chair section omits the returned lead-in “The revenue arithmetic is decisive:”. These are small presentation differences, but the originals should not be advertised as byte-exact captures. The final instruction “Do not assume either option will achieve the six-month revenue objective” was omitted from worker packets, although the objective and uncertainty were supplied and the outputs do not promise success.

These findings supplement and qualify the original audit. They do not change the preserved artifacts or call for another council run.
