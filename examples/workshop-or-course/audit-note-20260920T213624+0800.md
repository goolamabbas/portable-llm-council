## Findings

### 1. Arithmetic

Pass, with one qualification.

- Workshop capacity revenue: 15 × $150 = $2,250.
- Monthly workshop seat requirement: $3,000 ÷ $150 = 20 seats.
- Monthly course sales requirement: $3,000 ÷ $120 = 25 purchases.
- Six-week time capacity: 6 × 8 = 48 hours.
- Three-week time capacity: 3 × 8 = 24 hours.
- Workshop estimate: 18 + 4 = 22 hours.
- Course budget share: $1,200 ÷ $1,500 = 80%.
- Ten full-price pilot payments: 10 × $150 = $1,500 gross.

Qualification: the reviewer and chair calculate approximately 32 visits as 4% of 800 and then approximately 31% purchase conversion as 10 ÷ 32. The scenario says the list has 800 subscribers and that the 4% rate is measured against delivered emails, but it does not say all 800 messages were delivered or that each click equals one sales-page visitor. The figures are useful illustrations only if those assumptions hold. The original outputs appropriately use “roughly” and “implies,” but the report should not be read as establishing a measured 32-visit funnel.

### 2. Reviewer preference counts

Pass.

- Strongest response: E received 3 preferences; A received 2.
- Biggest blind spot: C received all 5 selections.
- The HTML report states these counts correctly.
- The chair did not treat the tally as proof and instead justified the recommendation from capacity, budget, paid-demand uncertainty, and recurring-revenue requirements.

### 3. Claimed agreement versus actual answers

Pass.

All five advisors recommend against building the self-paced course first and favor a paid live pilot or presale as the first validation step. All distinguish initial paid validation from proof of sustainable $3,000 monthly revenue. The chair accurately preserves the main disagreement: the delivery threshold and treatment of middling demand vary across answers.

The chair's statement that “some advisors would deliver with 8–10 buyers; one would consider 4–7; others require ten” is faithful to the returned answers. The final ten-payment threshold is the chair's judgment, not a claim of unanimity.

### 4. Unsupported assertions and assumptions stated as facts

Pass with disclosed cautions.

- No external market fact was introduced, and the scenario is consistently described as hypothetical.
- The 32-visit and 31% funnel calculations depend on full delivery to all 800 subscribers and clicks corresponding to distinct sales-page visits; those assumptions are not established by the scenario.
- The recommendation that ten payments is “sounder” is a reasoned judgment based on limited time slack, not a verified empirical threshold.
- “Excessive acquisition cost,” “material refunds,” “manageable support,” and “credible” asynchronous demand are useful decision concepts but remain unquantified. They should be defined before operational use.
- Team licences, templates, referrals, and new-buyer acquisition are possibilities or test ideas, not established demand.

No silent corrections were made to the original chair or worker outputs.

### 5. Proceed, change, and stop conditions

Pass.

- Proceed: 10 full-price payments, firm cutoff, remaining work still within 24 hours and no week above eight hours.
- Change: 6–9 payments, diagnose and change one variable, then run one bounded second presale.
- Stop: fewer than 6 payments on the first test, or failure of two clear full-price tests to reach the delivery threshold; also stop scaling for weak outcomes, material refunds, excessive support, individualized-consulting dependence, or threat to client work.

The ranges do not overlap. The two-test rule is consistent with the immediate stop rule: fewer than six ends the offer immediately; six to nine permits one revised test; a second failure to reach ten ends it.

### 6. Time, budget, and client-work constraints

Pass.

The recommendation explicitly addresses the three-week live window, six-week course window, 48-hour total capacity, 24-hour three-week capacity, 22-hour workshop estimate, $1,500 ceiling, $300 pilot cap, and the requirement not to displace existing client work. It treats optimistic estimates as a risk and requires moving or cancelling the event if preparation no longer fits.

The first step is bounded to the next week's eight hours and prohibits substantive production before the payment gate.
