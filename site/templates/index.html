The method

A recommendation with the discussion behind it.

Each stage gives you another way to examine the decision.

01

Five perspectives

Five separate advisors receive the same brief. Each explores it through a different lens, from fragile assumptions to practical execution.

02

Five blind reviews

Answers are shuffled and stripped of explicit role labels. Five fresh reviewers assess the arguments without the mapping or each other’s reviews.

03

One synthesis

A separate chair weighs the answers and reviews, explains agreement and disagreement, and recommends a course of action and a first step.

When to use it

Real stakes. Competing priorities.

Use it for product direction, pricing, hiring, positioning, or implementation strategy when the tradeoffs deserve a closer look.

Provide your objective, options, constraints, evidence, and important unknowns. For routine questions, a direct answer may be enough. When a small experiment can settle the uncertainty, run that experiment.

Keep the limits in view

Different perspectives do not create independent factual evidence. Agreement does not establish truth. Anonymous labels cannot conceal every stylistic clue.

A full council uses eleven worker runs, with more time and tokens than a direct answer. Results depend on the model, brief, and separate worker contexts. Incomplete rounds should be reported as incomplete.
