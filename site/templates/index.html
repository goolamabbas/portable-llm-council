Portable LLM Council · Why use it?

Has your AI examined the tradeoffs—or followed your framing?

When you ask an AI assistant for advice, it can be difficult to tell whether it has examined the tradeoffs or simply followed your framing.

Portable LLM Council is an open-source skill that helps you explore a business or technical decision through several perspectives before receiving a recommendation.

Five advisors examine the decision. Five fresh reviewers assess their answers. A separate chair brings the reasoning together, so you can inspect the assumptions, disagreements, and conditions behind the recommendation.

See how it works ↓
The method

Give the decision a structured examination.

Five thinking perspectives. Five blind reviews. One recommendation you can inspect.

01

Examine the decision from different angles

Five separate advisors receive the same brief. Each has a distinct assignment: question fragile assumptions, revisit the underlying objective, explore overlooked upside, expose unclear reasoning, or test practical feasibility.

02

Put the arguments through a separate review

After the advisors finish, their answers are shuffled and stripped of explicit role labels.

Five fresh reviewers receive the same anonymous answers, without the identity mapping or each other’s reviews. Each identifies the strongest argument, the biggest blind spot, and anything the answers collectively missed.

03

Turn the discussion into a course of action

A separate chair receives the original brief, named advisor answers, and reviews. It explains agreement and disagreement, weighs the arguments, and recommends a course of action with one concrete first step.

Strong reasoning can outweigh the majority.

From discussion to action

Leave with a recommendation you can examine.

See what the council recommends, why it recommends it, and where the advisors disagree.

Use the report to question the assumptions behind the recommendation, identify what still needs checking, and decide whether the proposed next step fits your circumstances.

The transcript lets you revisit the individual answers and reviews when you want to understand how the recommendation was reached.

  • Issues raised during review.
  • One concrete next step.

When to use it

Use it when the tradeoffs deserve the time.

The council is intended for consequential business and technical decisions: product direction, pricing, hiring, positioning, or an implementation strategy.

Give it your objective, alternatives, constraints, evidence, and important unknowns. Those details give each perspective something concrete to examine.

For routine questions, a direct answer may be enough. When a small experiment can resolve the uncertainty, that experiment may be the more useful next step.

Keep the limits in view

Different perspectives do not create independent factual evidence. Agreement does not establish truth, and anonymous labels cannot conceal every stylistic clue.

A full council uses eleven worker runs, which can take more time and tokens than a direct answer. Results depend on the model, the decision brief, and the environment’s ability to maintain separate worker contexts. If a required round cannot finish, the result should be marked incomplete.

Bring your own decision

Run the same method in your working environment.

Portable LLM Council combines one shared skill with native agent definitions for Codex, Cursor, Command Code, Grok Build, OpenCode, Factory Droid, and Claude Code.

The shared skill defines the method. The native definitions provide the advisor, reviewer, and chair roles for your environment. Together, they make the workflow reusable without having to assemble each assignment yourself.

Choose your environment, follow its installation guidance, and verify discovery before your first run.
