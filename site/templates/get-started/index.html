04 · Give it your decision

A good brief gives every perspective something to examine.

Use the llm-council skill to evaluate this decision.

Decision: [What must I decide, and by when?]
Objective: [What outcome matters most?]
Options: [What alternatives am I considering?]
Constraints: [Time, budget, people, and commitments.]
Evidence: [What do I know, and how do I know it?]
Unknowns: [What am I assuming or still need to learn?]

Recommend a course of action, explain what evidence would change it,
and give me one concrete first step. Distinguish facts from assumptions.

A complete run uses five advisors, five fresh reviewers, and one separate chair. Expect an HTML report and a Markdown transcript. Read the recommendation alongside its assumptions and limitations.

Check your first completed run.

Look for five advisor answers, five fresh reviews, one chair verdict, and both the HTML report and Markdown transcript. Check that the report reflects the returned answers. Incomplete rounds or an inability to preserve separate review contexts must be disclosed.

A full council takes more calls and potentially more time and cost than a direct answer. If delegation or fresh review contexts are unavailable, the run should be marked incomplete.
