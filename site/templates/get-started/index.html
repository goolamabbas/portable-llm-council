04 · Give it your decision

How much does the council need to know?

A decision brief is a short description of the choice you face and what matters to you. A few sentences can be enough. Include any limits that would rule an option out.

A paid pilot or the full product?

Should we run a paid pilot or build the full product first? We have two developers and eight weeks to find a customer problem worth solving. Six prospects described the same problem. None has agreed to pay. We’d have to support the pilot by hand, and building the full product would take most of those eight weeks. We can’t hire this quarter.

That’s enough to start. The staffing limit makes the tradeoff concrete. The interviews give the council evidence that the problem exists. Whether anyone will pay is a separate question the pilot could test.

Help me draft my brief

“Our weekends fill up. Should I hire someone?” is enough to start a draft. Are you turning away profitable bookings, trying to get some weekends back, or both? Your answer could change the recommendation. If you haven’t counted lost bookings, leave that figure open. If it would decide the question, check your bookings before running the council.

Paste the prompt below with your rough notes. You get to review the draft first.

Help me turn this into a neutral decision brief. Use what I’ve told you, flag assumptions, and ask only about missing details that could change the choice. Show me the brief and wait for my go-ahead before running the council.

My question and what I know so far: [Add your rough notes here.]

Run the council on my brief

If you drafted the brief with your assistant, review it and reply, “Run the llm-council skill on this brief.” If you already know what you want to ask, fill in the template below.

Use the llm-council skill to evaluate this decision.

Decision: [What must I decide, and by when?]
Objective: [What outcome matters most?]
Options: [What alternatives am I considering?]
Constraints: [Time, budget, people, and commitments.]
Evidence: [What do I know, and how do I know it?]
Unknowns: [What am I assuming or still need to learn?]

Recommend a course of action, explain what evidence would change it,
and give me one concrete first step. Distinguish facts from assumptions.

A complete run uses five advisors, five fresh reviewers, and one separate chair. Expect an HTML report and a Markdown transcript. Read the recommendation alongside its assumptions and limitations.

05 · Check the result

Check your first completed run.

By default, your assistant saves both files in the current project folder. Open the HTML report in your browser to read the recommendation. Open the Markdown transcript in a text editor to see the five advisor answers, five fresh reviews, and chair’s verdict. Check that the report reflects the returned answers. Incomplete rounds or an inability to preserve separate review contexts must be disclosed.

A full council uses 11 agent runs, so allow more time and tokens than for a direct answer.
